Foody

How I debugged a bilingual nutrition app: 50 bugs, 3 root causes

Role: designer, product owner and developer, built with AI assistance. Stack: Python, Flask, JavaScript, Tailwind, CSV data, optional Gemini or OpenAI.
Live appCode on request

Watch on YouTube

The problem

People who want to eat to a calorie or protein target, in English or Spanish, juggle recipes, nutrition tables and a shopping list in different places. I wanted one app that turns the ingredients you pick, your diet and your targets into recipes and a priced grocery list.

What I built

A rule-based “Core Engine” builds recipes and checks nutrition with no AI call. Gemini or OpenAI can enhance a recipe, and if the call fails (no key, quota, invalid key) the app falls back to the Core Engine and says why in plain language. Everything is bilingual. Saved recipes and the profile stay in the browser, so there is no account and no infrastructure cost. The grocery list groups by store, converts currency and checks a budget.

What went wrong, and what it taught me

I kept a bug diary. It reached 50 entries, and when I grouped them, three causes explained almost all of them:

  1. The same information lived in more than one place. Translations were stored in three places; portions lived in both a dropdown and the recipe object; store settings and city data could drift apart. Fix: one source of truth for each.
  2. Silent failures. A wrong key, a category name that never matched, or a store missing from a config fell back quietly instead of failing. Fix: match real names, and make failures visible.
  3. Patch-style editing. Find-and-replace removed a closing tag or brace, and one early error cascaded into everything after it. Fix: replace whole blocks, fix the first error first, hard-refresh before judging.

Two examples

  • Spanish titles had the wrong word order. The title was built from a fixed English pattern. I redesigned it as “X con Y, estilo italiano”, so no gender agreement is needed.
  • Calories above the limit. Per-serving calories were divided by portions a second time, so the cap never triggered. The fix removed the extra division and scaled all nutrients together.

Decisions I would defend

Protein target wins over the calorie limit, with a visible conflict flag. Saved recipes are snapshots in the language they were saved in. “Tabs are for browsing, the Lab is for editing.” Prices are labeled estimates, and a fake cooking-time filter was removed instead of kept.

Known limits and next steps

The grocery list shows recipe quantities (“4 oz of tuna”) where people buy packages (“1 can”). The data model has no packaging layer; the plan is a package-size table with round-up. Next: link-based recipe sharing, then accounts and a database.

What this shows

Finding root causes instead of patching symptoms, designing for failure (the AI fallback), and keeping an honest record of mistakes.
