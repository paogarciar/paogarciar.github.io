AnalytixTool

A sales forecast that tests itself before you trust it

Role: designer, analyst and developer, built with AI assistance. Stack: Python, Flask, pandas, NumPy, optional Gemini.
Live appCode on request

Watch on YouTube

The problem

Small teams export sales to a spreadsheet and draw a trend line. They rarely know how wrong it is likely to be, so they cannot decide how much stock or budget to commit. I wanted a forecast that shows its own error, and a SWOT that turns the numbers into actions.

What I built

Upload a CSV with a date and a sales column (product and region optional). The app checks the data, then runs a tournament of forecasting models. Each is tested by hiding the last 12 months and predicting them. The best model wins, and a simpler model wins when the result is a near tie. The chart shows the next 12 months with a likely range and a “How this forecast works” panel. Other tabs cover key numbers, products, regions, cohorts, an ad-impact view, what-if and goal tools, and a SWOT drafted by Gemini with a labeled rule-based fallback.

The bug that mattered

My first forecast missed by about 11%, worse than the naive guess. The large November and December peaks were tilting the trend line upward. I changed the model to estimate trend and seasonality together, repeated three times, and the miss dropped to about 2.3% on that version. The app now shows the baseline comparison on screen, so a visitor can see whether the model beats a simple guess.

Judgment calls

  • Revenue definition. Only completed orders count (cancelled, returned and pending excluded): $144.2M, not the $177M in the dataset's summary file.
  • Mismatched files. The item-level file did not add up to the orders file, so I split each order's revenue across its items.
  • Currencies. Seven currencies on the same scale, treated as reported; the README says so.
  • Rising and declining products. Swings like +250% came from tiny bases, so the list covers only products of at least median size.
  • Honest limits. Price sensitivity is an association, and flat 60% yearly repurchase suggests the sample data is generated. The page says so.

Limits and next steps

The models are written in plain NumPy because statsmodels and Prophet were not available, so “Auto-SARIMA” is SARIMA-style. The ad-spend data in the sample is simulated and labeled. Next: column mapping for uploads and saved views.

What this shows

Test against a baseline, explain the error, and state what the data cannot tell you.
