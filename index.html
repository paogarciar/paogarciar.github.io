Foody

A bilingual nutrition app that turns your ingredients and targets into recipes and a priced grocery list

PythonFlaskJavaScriptTailwindGemini / OpenAI (optional)

Foody plans meals the way a nutritionist and a chef would together. Users choose their diet, favorite cuisines, calorie and protein targets, and budget per portion to generate four custom recipe options, complete with nutrition breakdowns, cost estimates, cooking steps, and a grocery list. Under the hood, a deterministic rule-based engine builds the baseline recipes while AI enhances them, falling back gracefully with transparent error messaging if an API call fails. The entire experience switches instantly between English and Spanish, refined through a 50-entry bug diary that isolated and fixed the three core root causes behind almost every failure.

Anti-diabetic drug sales

Forecasting with a rolling backtest

Can we predict how much diabetes medication a country will need next year? Using Australian monthly sales data, I tested whether the series was stationary (ADF), made it stationary with seasonal differencing, and ran a backtest over four consecutive fiscal years (2004–2008). Every model was retrained and scored on each year in turn. A multiplicative Holt-Winters model came out on top every time, with forecasts within 3–9% of actual sales. That is the kind of accuracy supply-chain and pharma planners can use for inventory decisions.

Best markets to advertise

Guided project · Where should an e-learning company spend its ad budget?

Instead of paying for a new survey, I repurposed freeCodeCamp's survey of more than 18,000 new coders. I first checked that the audience matched the product (86% wanted web or mobile skills), then measured both market size and how much people actually spend per month on learning to code. The raw averages were misleading: Australia looked like the top spender until a handful of $5,000+/month answers were investigated and removed. After cleaning, the US was the clear winner on both size and spend. The UK fell below the $59 price point, and India vs. Canada came down to market size vs. spend per person.

Amazon stock price analysis

Building a dataset and testing economic indicators

Does the economy move Amazon's stock? I built my own dataset from scratch, pulling Amazon's full price history since its 1997 IPO and joining it with GDP, CPI, inflation, unemployment and retail-sales data from the World Bank and US government sources. A head-to-head with eBay shows Amazon overtaking its rival around 2016 and growing exponentially from there, while eBay's stock was the more volatile of the two. Feature-importance analysis pointed to consumer prices (CPI) as the strongest macro signal, which fits a company that sells household goods. I then stress-tested forecasting models on the hardest window possible: the 2020 pandemic boom and the 2022 sell-off.
