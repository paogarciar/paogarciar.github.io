AgentSpark

An interview that writes your AI prompt

Role: designer, product owner and developer, built with AI assistance. Stack: Python, Flask, JavaScript, optional Gemini polish.
Live appCode on request

Free server: the first load can take up to 90 seconds.

Watch on YouTube

The problem

Most people write AI-agent prompts from a blank page. Prompts come out vague, miss safety rules, and behave differently on Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and other tools. Prompt generators tend to be long forms that people abandon.

What I built

A chat that asks one question at a time and builds a structured prompt for the platform you choose. It starts with your field (business, data, software, writing, teaching and research, personal systems and home life, or other), so every question offers fitting presets. Quick mode takes 8 questions; Full mode takes 25. Every question has presets, “Write my own” and “Skip”.

The prompt is assembled in the browser from templates, with no AI call, so it is instant, free and private. Claude and the API get XML tags; other tools get markdown. ChatGPT's 8,000-character limit is handled by trimming examples first, then formatting. A fixed safety section always comes first, and a conflict check catches contradictory answers (for example “very short” with “step by step in detail”) before the prompt is built.

Optionally, a visitor can paste their own Gemini key to polish the wording. The key is never stored or logged. Prompts can be saved in the browser, exported as a markdown file and imported back.

Design decisions from testing it myself

  • Single-choice questions advance on their own; multi-choice questions use Next or Enter, so nobody clicks Continue every time.
  • A separate question for the reading level of the final result, because the agent's voice and the reader's level are different things.
  • “Missing information” is multiple choice (stop and ask, or fill a template), not free text.
  • The mission question shows a live sentence so the user sees what the prompt will say.
  • A “words or habits to ban” step, because style complaints such as emojis and slang are a common reason people rewrite prompts.

Limits and next steps

The presets are my judgment and have not been tested with many users. I have not yet run a formal comparison of prompts built here against hand-written ones. The first test I would run is the same task with and without a built prompt.

What this shows

Product thinking (reducing form fatigue), designing for platform differences and failure cases, and being honest about what has and has not been validated.
